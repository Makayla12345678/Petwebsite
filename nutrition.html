🥗 Nutrition

Feeding Your Pet Well

Good nutrition is the foundation of a long, healthy life. Here's what you need to know.

Nutrition Matters

What you feed your pet directly affects their energy, coat condition, weight, and longevity. A good diet reduces the risk of obesity, diabetes, dental disease, and organ problems. The right food depends on species, age, size, and health conditions — when in doubt, ask your vet.

🐶

Feeding Dogs

Dogs need a balanced diet of protein, fat, carbohydrates, vitamins, and minerals. Feed twice daily for adults; puppies need three to four meals. Choose an AAFCO-approved food appropriate for your dog's life stage. Avoid grapes, raisins, onions, chocolate, and xylitol — all are toxic to dogs.

🐱

Feeding Cats

Cats are obligate carnivores and require animal protein to survive. A diet combining wet and dry food is widely recommended. Wet food supports hydration, which is critical for urinary health. Never feed dogs' food to cats — it lacks the nutrients cats specifically need, such as taurine.

🐰

Small Animals

Rabbits thrive on unlimited hay, fresh leafy greens, and limited pellets. Guinea pigs need vitamin C daily (they can't synthesise it themselves) — bell peppers and leafy greens are great sources. Hamsters do well on a commercial mix supplemented with fresh vegetables in small amounts.

💧

Hydration

Fresh, clean water should always be available. Cats especially are prone to under-drinking — consider a cat fountain, which encourages drinking. Signs of dehydration include lethargy, dry gums, and skin that doesn't spring back when gently pinched. Dehydration is a veterinary emergency.
